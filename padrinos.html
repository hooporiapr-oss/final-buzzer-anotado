Every padrino raises what one team point is worth

Each padrino pledges whatever they like, and their pledges add together. That total is what one point your team scores is worth — and it goes up every time somebody says yes.

Your Point Value

Every padrino’s pledge, added together. That is what one team point is worth. Ten cents from one, a dollar from another — it all goes into the same number, and the number only ever goes up.

It is the one part that is in your hands before the game. The team sets the score by playing; you set what a point is worth by asking.

About the ceiling

A padrino can set a ceiling — the most they will pay however the game goes. Plenty do, and it is a sensible thing to do. The system applies it, so what they are told at the end is never more than the number they chose.

And about the $5 floor

A pledge is what it comes to at the end: what they pledged a point, times the team’s points. If that works out under $5, the pledge closes — nothing is owed and no notice is sent for it. In a 60-point game even ten cents a point comes to $6, so this only bites on a very small pledge or a low-scoring game.

This is the number to grow. The team scores what it scores whether you found two padrinos or twenty, so every padrino you find raises what each team point is worth — in this game and in every one after it.

Ten cents already counts

Ten cents a point is a real pledge — in a 60-point game that is six dollars. So is a dollar a point. People give what suits them, and none of them need to know what anyone else gave: they only ever see their own.

So do not decide for them. Ask everyone, let each one choose their own number, and watch what a point is worth climb.

One game, and every point counts

A padrino pledges on one game your team already has on the schedule. Pledging closes when the program publishes the final score. Win or lose, every point counts.

Before the game Share the game’s link One link for the game, to as many people as you can reach. Each one decides how much to pledge a point, and whether to set a ceiling.
The game Play it The team plays. When it is over, the program publishes the final score.
After Everyone sees the result Each padrino gets one email with the final score and what their pledge came to, and pays the program directly — no payment link and no reminders. If the score is ever corrected, they get the correction.

There is nobody to lose to. A padrino pays on the points the team scored, win or lose.

Who to ask

A padrino is anyone who would be glad you asked. Most families find ten without leaving the family.

Parents, and both sets of grandparents
Aunts, uncles, older cousins
Godparents, and your parents' close friends
Neighbours you actually know
A coach, a teacher, a former teacher
Family who live far away — distance does not matter
A business your family knows — where your parents work, the shop on the corner

The ones who live furthest away are often the quickest to say yes. They cannot come to anything, and this is something they can be part of.

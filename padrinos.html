You score. Your padrinos decide what every point is worth.

How far you get in your run is down to how much you practise. What each of those points is worth is down to how many people you asked — and you can start that today, before you play at all.

Every padrino raises what one of your points is worth

Each padrino pledges whatever they like, and their pledges add together. That total is what one point you score is worth — and it goes up every time somebody says yes.

Your Padrino Point Value

Every padrino’s pledge, added together. That is what one of your points is worth. Ten cents from one, two dollars from another — it all goes into the same number, and the number only ever goes up.

It is the one part that is in your hands before you play. You set the number by practising; you set what a point is worth by asking.

About the ceiling

A padrino can set a ceiling — the most they will pay however the game goes. Plenty do, and it is a sensible thing to do. The system applies it, so what they are told at the end is never more than the number they chose.

And about the $5 floor

A pledge is what it comes to at the end: what they pledged a point, times your points. If that works out under $5, the pledge closes out — nothing is owed and nothing reaches your program. On a 150-point run even a dime comes to $15, so this only bites on a small pledge and a short run.

This is the number to grow. You score what you score whether you found two padrinos or twenty, so every padrino you find raises what each point you score is worth — in this run and in every one after it.

There is no amount too small to ask for

Ten cents a point is a real pledge — on a 150-point run that is fifteen dollars. So is two dollars a point. People give what suits them, and none of them need to know what anyone else gave: they only ever see their own.

So do not decide for them. Ask everyone, let each one choose their own number, and watch what a point is worth climb.

One run, and it counts

A padrino pledges on you, you take your run inside the window your program set, and he pays the program afterwards. Pledging on you closes the moment you start your official run — and you only get one, because the database refuses a second.

Before your run Share your link One link, as many people as you can reach. Each one decides what to pledge on your run, and whether to set a ceiling.
Your run Play it Every round is worth more than the last — round nine banks nine points — so the number at the buzzer is bigger than the run felt. Nobody writes it down: the game records it as you play.
After Everyone sees the result Each padrino gets one email with your points and what their pledge came to. They pay the program directly — no payment link, and no reminders after it.

There is nobody to lose to. A padrino pays on the points you scored, however the run goes — and nobody has to take your word for the number, because the game recorded it.

Who to ask

A padrino is anyone who would be glad you asked. Most players find ten without leaving the family.

Parents, and both sets of grandparents
Aunts, uncles, older cousins
Godparents, and your parents' close friends
Neighbours you actually know
A coach, a teacher, a former teacher
Family who live far away — distance does not matter
A business your family knows — where your parents work, the shop on the corner

The ones who live furthest away are often the quickest to say yes. They cannot come to anything, and this is something they can be part of.
